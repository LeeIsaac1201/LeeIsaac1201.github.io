Challenges

Smaller problems solved along the way — the friction that doesn't make it into a full case study but still shaped how I think.

01

Scoping under real constraints

HomeHero was originally scoped to include a full administrator role, a separate dashboard for managing users, providers, and disputes. I cut it to keep the capstone timeline realistic, but that meant defending the decision to my supervisor rather than just quietly dropping a whole role from the system, and rethinking which administrative functions, if any, needed to survive as manual workarounds instead.

02

Reconstructing decisions after the fact

I did not keep a running design log while building HomeHero, so by the time I sat down to write this case study I had to reverse-engineer my own reasoning from old Figma versions and half-remembered supervisor feedback. A few decisions I could not fully justify anymore, which told me more about my process than I expected.

03

Designing for a domain I am still learning

The health log timeline and entry flow is where pet owners record things like vet visits, medication, and symptoms, none of which I track myself. Getting the research-to-prototype cycle right meant sitting with how people actually log that information before assuming what a "better" timeline should look like.

04

Redesigning something I already built

PawsBase is an app I developed with a team, and the original health log screens were built with my own code decisions baked in. Coming back to redesign the same flow meant separating what I had built for engineering convenience from what the entry flow actually needed for the person using it, a harder split than it sounds.

05

Privacy as a first-class concern, not an afterthought

Data privacy was not part of HomeHero's original scope. It showed up as a gap partway through, once I started thinking about what a service marketplace actually exposes about both homeowners and providers. I did not have room for a full redesign at that point, so I had to work out what could be addressed now versus flagged honestly as future work.

06

Running a full design process on evenings only

Testing PawsBase with real users meant fitting a full research-to-prototype cycle, planning, wireframing, and actual usability sessions, around HomeHero and coursework. The process could not be shortened without losing the "tested with real users" part, so the time had to come from somewhere else.
