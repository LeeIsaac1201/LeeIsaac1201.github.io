Challenges

Smaller problems solved along the way — the friction that doesn't make it into a full case study but still shaped how I think.

01

Scoping under real constraints

Reducing HomeHero's feature set to fit capstone deadlines meant deciding what to cut without losing the core user value — a trade-off I had to justify, not just make.

02

Reconstructing decisions after the fact

HomeHero was built iteratively without a formal design log, so writing it up for this portfolio meant piecing together why each decision was made, after the fact.

03

Designing for a domain I'm still learning

Approaching a UX redesign for PawsBase meant researching how pet owners actually track health records before assuming what "better" looked like.

04

Redesigning something I already built

Since I developed the original PawsBase screens, separating "how I built it" from "how I'd design it properly" took a conscious step back before I could see it with fresh eyes.

05

Privacy as a first-class concern, not an afterthought

Partway through HomeHero I realized data privacy needed real design attention — not a mention in the docs — and had to work out how to fold that in without a full redesign.

06

Running a full design process on evenings only

Compressing research, wireframing, and testing for PawsBase into a few evening hours a week meant being disciplined about scope — without cutting the process short.
